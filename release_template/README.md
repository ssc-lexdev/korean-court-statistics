# Korean Judicial Yearbook Court Statistics, 2002–2025

National caseload series, a court-level **filing** panel, detailed civil/family/criminal series, and case-duration distributions, digitized from the 24 editions (2002–2025) of the *Judicial Yearbook* (사법연감) published by the National Court Administration of Korea.

> Version 1.0.0 — generated by `make_release.py`. DOI: [10.5281/zenodo.24470048](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24470048). Code: <https://github.com/ssc-lexdev/korean-court-statistics> (release v1.0.0). Authors: Sungsoo Choi and Taeyeon Oh (contact); see `CITATION.cff`.

## Key conventions
- **`year` = statistical year.** The edition year of the Yearbook equals the calendar year of the statistics it reports (verified against the tables). The edition a value was taken from is in `source_edition`.
- **Units:** cases, except criminal tables, which count **persons (defendants)**; duration `mean_months` is in months. Every fact table has a `unit` column.
- **Court panel = filings only.** No court-level dispositions or pending.
- **Totals coexist with courts.** In `court_panel.csv`, rows with `unit_type` in (`national_total`, `group_total`, `district_total`) are printed totals. Filter them out before summing. The printed district totals differ by table: `*_subtotal` (소계) in the collegiate, single-judge, criminal and family tables, `*_area_total` (관내합계, including si/gun courts) in the small-claims table. Court rows (high, main, branch and si/gun courts) are not double counted: excluding `dup_flag` rows, they sum exactly to the national total in every table and year (checked).
- **Identifiers** are ASCII lowercase snake_case and stable across releases (`court_id_h`, `table_id`, `category_id`, …). Korean labels are kept in the lookup tables and in `source_*_ko` fields. **The Korean source labels are authoritative; English labels are provided for convenience.**
- **English labels** are official names, systematic patterns or author labels; the source is stated (`court_en_source`). Labels whose English is a functional translation (e.g. 본안 = "contentious/main", 즉결 = immediate summary trial, 약식 = summary order) carry a `translation_note_en` in `categories.csv`, `tables.csv` and `domains.csv`. English court names are current names; historical Korean labels are kept in `source_court_label_ko` and `harmonized_from_ko`.
- **Court names in English:** official names from the Supreme Court of Korea English website where available (`court_en_source = official_scourt_english_site`). Branch courts and si/gun courts (시·군법원) are named with the terms of the official English translation of the Court Organization Act ("Branch Court", "Si/Gun court") plus the place name in Revised Romanization (`pattern_court_organization_act`). Same-named units (e.g., two Goseong Gun Courts; Gwangju Si Court in Gyeonggi vs Gwangju District Court) are distinguished by `court_id_h` and `parent_court_id_h`.
- **Encoding:** `data/` = UTF-8 **without BOM** (canonical); `csv_excel/` = identical files in UTF-8 with BOM for Excel. No thousands separators; missing = empty.

## Using the data over time
The dataset links courts and case types across 2002–2025, but **it is not definition-invariant**: choose an aggregate that matches the question, and respect institutional and jurisdictional breaks.

**Courts (`court_events.csv`, `courts.csv`, `coverage.csv`)**
- **A `court_id_h` is an analytical unit.** It keeps its ID through renaming and conversion. For example, `incheon_fc` holds the Incheon District Court's family cases before the 2016 conversion. It is not always the legal institution of that name.
- **`court_events.csv`** lists 57 events as machine-readable links: establishment, branch split, high-court split, bankruptcy court creation, family-court conversion, renaming, abolition and jurisdiction transfer. Each row gives `from_court_id_h` → `to_court_id_h`, the unit whose series breaks (`affected_parent_court_id_h`), the case scope and a `recommended_user_action`.
- **A parent court's series falls when a branch or specialized court is created.** This is a reallocation, not a workload change. For a stable territory, sum a district court and all units with that `parent_court_id_h` (district-wide). Across the six branch splits in 2003–2022, main-court civil first-instance filings fall 15–32% in the split year. The district-wide share of national filings changes by at most 0.6 percentage points, except Daegu 2007 (−1.3 points, after a 2006 small-claims peak). High courts: compare `seoul_hc` up to 2018 with `seoul_hc + suwon_hc` from 2019.
- **Bankruptcy courts** (Seoul 2017; Suwon and Busan 2023) appear in civil tables with small counts. Add them to their former parent court for civil series only.
- **Printed zeros before establishment:** the Yearbook's 10-year tables print current courts with 0 for years before they existed. These rows have `pre_establishment = True` and `coverage_status = present_before_establishment`. Treat them as "not yet existing", not as zero filings.

**Case types and jurisdiction (`structural_breaks.csv`, `break_flag`)**
- **Civil first instance:** the split between collegiate, single-judge and small-claims tables follows monetary thresholds. These changed on 2015-02-13 (collegiate threshold KRW 100m → 200m), on 2017-01-01 (small-claims ceiling KRW 20m → 30m) and on 2022-03-01 (KRW 200m → 500m). Affected rows carry the break codes in `break_flag` (';'-separated).
  - For a split-free series use the derived `civil_main_1st_total` (collegiate + single + small claims). Nationally it equals the printed first-instance filings in `detail.csv` in every year.
- **Criminal first instance:** the collegiate/single split follows Court Organization Act Art. 32(1). Its offence-specific amendments (2016–2022) are registered without row flags. Two level shifts in collegiate filings have no identified cause (`confidence = observed_only`): 2012 and 2024–2025. Use `criminal_trial_1st_total` for a split-free series.
- **`confidence`** in `structural_breaks.csv` is `verified` (statute text or Yearbook note), `suspected` or `observed_only`.

```python
p = pd.read_csv("data/court_panel.csv"); courts = pd.read_csv("data/courts.csv")
parent = dict(zip(courts.court_id_h, courts.parent_court_id_h))
c = p[(p.table_id == "civil_main_1st_total") & p.unit_type.isin(["main_court", "branch", "si_gun_court"]) & p.dup_flag.isna()].copy()
c["district"] = [cid if t == "main_court" else parent[cid] for cid, t in zip(c.court_id_h, c.unit_type)]
district_wide = c.groupby(["district", "year"]).value.sum().unstack(0)   # stable across branch splits
```

## Duration tables (`duration.csv`): definitions
Source tables: 처리기간별 누년비교표-심급별 (disposition-time year-on-year tables by instance) for civil main cases, family litigation and criminal trials.
- **What is counted:** cases (criminal: persons) **disposed** in the year, by time from filing to disposition. "처리기간별 건수는 판결뿐 아니라 모든 처리건수를 포함하였음" — all dispositions, not only judgments (table note, e.g. 2025 ed. family table; 2002–2007 ed. civil table).
- **Start point and suspensions:** "처리기간은 접수일로부터 계산하고 정지 중의 일수는 처리기간에 산입하지 아니하였다" — duration runs from the filing date, and days of suspension are excluded (General Notes, 일러두기 §1.가(3), 2016, 2018 and 2020–2025 editions). The note sits under civil/family/administrative/patent/election statistics. The criminal section of the notes has no separate rule, and the earlier editions' general notes are not part of the downloaded chapter files.
- **Per instance:** each instance (first instance, appeal, Supreme Court) is a separate row. We read "filing date" as the filing date at that instance; this is our inference, as no note says it explicitly. It is consistent with the Yearbook reporting "time to finality" separately, counted from first-instance filing to final disposition (확정사건 평균처리일수, from the 2018 ed.). Those time-to-finality statistics are **not** included in this dataset.
- **Bins** are mutually exclusive, and each label is an inclusive upper bound ("6월이내" = more than the previous bound, up to 6 months). The criminal tables have an extra 9-month bin. Counts sum to `total` (checked).
- **Mean (`mean_months`) is as printed.** Its method changes in 2007 (`break_flag = mean_months_bin_midpoint_2002_2006`):
  - **2002–2006:** approximated from bin representative values: 0.5, 1.5, …, 5.5, 7.5 (9-month bin), 9 or 10.5 (1-year bin), 18 and 30 months (2004/2006 ed. 사건개황 notes). Recomputing from the released bins reproduces all 70 printed means for 2002–2006.
  - **From 2007:** mean disposition days ÷ 30 ("평균처리일수를 30으로 나눈 수치", 2007 ed. 사건개황). Only about half of the later means are reproduced by the bin approximation (`validation/duration_mean_method_check.csv`).
  - **Comparing means across 2006/2007:** recompute both years from the bins.
- **Precision:** means from the 2025 Excel edition (statistical years 2021–2025) carry 4 decimals; earlier values carry 1 decimal as printed.
- **Other notes:** the civil single-judge row includes small claims from 2010 (`civil_single_incl_small_claims_2010`). In criminal Supreme Court tables, the collegiate row includes military-court cases (table note).

## Quick start (Python)
```python
import pandas as pd
p = pd.read_csv("data/court_panel.csv")
courts = pd.read_csv("data/courts.csv")
real = p[~p.unit_type.isin(["national_total", "group_total", "district_total"])]   # courts only
small_claims = real[real.table_id == "civil_main_1st_small_claims"].merge(
    courts[["court_id_h", "court_en", "parent_court_id_h"]], on="court_id_h")
national = pd.read_csv("data/national_series.csv")
filings = national.query("category_id == 'main_cases' and instance_id == 'all_instances' and metric_id == 'filings'")
```

## Source files (not included)
The Yearbook PDF and Excel files are **not redistributed** in this package. `data/source_manifest.csv` lists all 228 files: edition, title, file name, size, SHA-256 and download date.
- **Where to get them:** the files are listed on the Judicial Statistics Portal (`portal` column), which serves them on request from its listing page.
- **Retrieval script:** `scrape_scourt.py` uses Playwright to open the listing and click each row's Excel, PDF and description download buttons (`retrieval` column). It is provided with the code, but portal changes may break it.
- **Checking a download:** compare its SHA-256 with the manifest before rebuilding.
